Pacific Coast Highway: the real stops

Photo: Taylen Lundequam / Pexels

The single most useful thing to know before you book this drive isn't a stop. It's a direction. Drive south from San Francisco toward Los Angeles, and you spend the entire trip in the ocean-side lane. Drive it the other way and the ocean is on your passenger's side for most of the route, across the double yellow line from every pullout.

Beyond that, the drive is really five decisions about where to stop.

  1. San Francisco
    Start here and head south. This is what puts the ocean on your side of the car for the rest of the drive.
  2. Monterey & 17-Mile Drive
    A toll road through the Monterey Peninsula. The detour off Highway 1 is short, and most people who skip it regret it.
  3. Big Sur
    The stretch the highway is famous for. Bixby Bridge and McWay Falls are both roadside, no hiking required to see either.
  4. San Luis Obispo
    Roughly the midpoint of the drive, and a sensible place to break the trip into two days instead of pushing straight through.
  5. Santa Barbara
    The last coastal stop before the highway turns inland and traffic toward Los Angeles picks up.

None of these stops require a reservation or a specific arrival time. The drive rewards flexibility more than planning. The one thing worth arranging ahead of time is the car itself: a lot of the value of this route is in unplanned pullouts, which means you want a rental you're not worried about parking on gravel shoulders.
