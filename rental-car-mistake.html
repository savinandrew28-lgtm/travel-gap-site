The rental car mistake that costs the most

Photo: Berna / Pexels

The expensive moment on a road trip usually isn't the booking. It's the rental counter, where coverage gets offered under time pressure: a queue behind you, a flight you just got off, an agent running through add-ons fast.

Two ways this goes wrong, in opposite directions:

Declining everything and hoping the credit card covers it. Card-issued rental coverage exists, but the terms vary a lot by card, by country, and by what kind of vehicle and trip you're on. Finding out it doesn't apply happens at the worst possible time: after something's already happened to the car.

Accepting everything at the counter without knowing what's already covered. This is the more common mistake, and the more expensive one. Desk-sold coverage is priced for a walk-up decision, not a planned one, and stacking it on top of coverage you already have elsewhere is pure margin for the counter, not protection for you.

The fix isn't complicated. It just has to happen before you're standing at the desk. Know what your card covers, what your own insurance covers, and what the rental company's own optional coverage actually adds on top of that, before the agent starts talking. Everything at that point becomes a five-second decision instead of a five-minute sales conversation.

The other way to sidestep the desk conversation entirely: settle the coverage question when you book, not when you arrive.
